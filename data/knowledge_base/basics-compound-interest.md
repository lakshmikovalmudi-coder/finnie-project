# Compound Interest

Source: https://www.investor.gov/financial-tools-calculators/calculators/compound-interest-calculator

Compound interest is interest earned on both your original money (the principal)
and on the interest it has already earned. Over time, this "interest on interest"
makes balances grow faster than simple interest, which only pays on the principal.

**Example.** $1,000 earning 6% per year:
- Simple interest after 10 years: $1,000 + 10 × $60 = $1,600.
- Compounded annually after 10 years: $1,000 × 1.06¹⁰ ≈ $1,791.

The three levers are the **rate**, the **time**, and **how often** interest
compounds. Time is usually the most powerful: starting ten years earlier can
matter more than contributing more later.

The "Rule of 72" is a quick estimate: divide 72 by the annual rate to get the
approximate years for money to double (72 ÷ 6 ≈ 12 years).
